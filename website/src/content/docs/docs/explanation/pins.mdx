---
title: How pins differ from tabs and Favorites
description: Why a pin has a page of its own, and why a pin goes back to its saved page only when you ask.
---

Sval has three ways to keep a site, each for a different kind of use.

- **Tabs.** Tabs hold what you're doing now. A tab belongs to one
  Workspace and lasts until you close it.
- **Favorites.** Favorites are links you keep, in folders in the Favorites
  menu. Opening a Favorite opens a new tab.
- **Pins.** Pins are for the few sites you live in, such as your mail and
  your calendar. Each pin is its own page, one click away in every
  Workspace.

## Pins and Favorites

Your pins and your Favorites are two separate lists. A site can be a pin, a
Favorite, both, or neither. Changing a pin never changes a Favorite, and
changing a Favorite never changes a pin. So on a pin's page, the
**Favorite** button shows only whether the page is in Favorites. Taking
the page out of Favorites leaves the pin where it is.

Pins sync, and you can rename them and keep them in pin folders. When you
export your Favorites, your pins go in a folder named "Pins" at the end.

## Its icon is its page

When you click a pin, you see the pin's own page. Sval doesn't open a new
tab each time. The pin's page never shows in the list of tabs, so the pin
always has a page to come back to.

## It goes back only when you ask

Sval never reloads a pin when you come back to it, because a reload would
throw away a half-written email. To send a pin back to its saved page, use
the refresh button on the pin's tile, or choose Back to saved page in the
pin's menu. The pin goes back by itself only when you close the pin, or
when Sval puts the pin to sleep because your computer runs low on memory.

The saved page never changes on its own either. It changes only when you
choose Edit address… or Save current page as this pin, so a pin can't end
up saving a sign-out page.

## It stays on its own site

When you follow a link in a pin to another site, the link opens in a Quick Peek
over the pin. A link to another address on the same site stays in the pin,
such as a link from app.fastmail.com to www.fastmail.com. The pin keeps
showing its own site, so the pin's icon always tells you what's behind it.
You can keep the page from the Quick Peek as a tab.

## Each Workspace has its own page

A pin shows in every Workspace unless you limit where it shows. Each
Workspace has its own page for the pin, signed in with that Workspace's
sign-ins. In an
isolated Workspace, the same Fastmail pin can show your work account while
your other Workspaces show your personal one.
