Fingerprinting protection limits what sites can learn about your browser and
device. Sval's defaults make you look like other Sval users and rarely break
sites. In Settings, you can protect more, or less.

You want sites to learn as little as possible about your computer, and you
accept that some sites might break.

<Screenshot name="docs-fingerprinting" frame="rounded" maxWidth={600} alt="Settings › Privacy and protection › Fingerprinting, with protection on in every window and Strict fingerprinting protection off" />

## Turn on Strict fingerprinting protection

1. Open Settings › Privacy and protection.
2. Under Fingerprinting, turn on **Strict fingerprinting protection**.

The line under the switch says how many strict items are on. The change
takes effect the next time a page loads.

## Change one protection

1. Open Settings › Privacy and protection.
2. Under Fingerprinting, open **Advanced**.
3. Select a group, such as **Graphics**.
4. Turn the protection you want on or off.

Each protection says what it hides and what it can break.

## Go back to Sval's defaults

1. Open Settings › Privacy and protection.
2. Under Fingerprinting, select **Restore Sval's defaults**.

Sval shows the message **Restored Sval's defaults.**, with **Undo**.

## Details

- **Fingerprinting protection.** Choose where protection applies: **In
  every window**, **Only in private windows**, or **Off**. It starts on In
  every window.
- **Strict fingerprinting protection.** This switch is off at first.
  Turning it on turns on the strict items, which protect you more and break
  more sites. When only some strict items are on, the switch reads Partly
  on. Turning the switch on then turns on the rest.
- **Tell sites all your languages.** The switch is off at first, so sites see
  only your first language.
- **Report the real CPU core count.** The switch is off at first, so each site
  sees a count of its own, between half your cores and all of them.
- **WebRTC (calls in the browser).** Choose **Allow calls**, **Only through
  your proxy**, or **Block**. It starts on Allow calls, and sites don't see
  your local network address.
- **Mozilla's compatibility fixes.** You'll find this switch in Advanced,
  under Compatibility. See
  [Turn on Mozilla's compatibility fixes](/docs/how-to/privacy/turn-on-mozillas-compatibility-fixes/).
- To turn protection off for one site, use the Privacy and Security button.
  See [Turn off protections for a site](/docs/how-to/privacy/turn-off-protections-for-a-site/).
