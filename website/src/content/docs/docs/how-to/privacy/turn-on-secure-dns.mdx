Secure DNS controls how Sval looks up the sites you visit. Out of the box,
Sval uses your network's DNS. You can have Sval use an encrypted resolver
instead.

You use public Wi-Fi at a café, and you don't want the network to see which
sites you look up.

<Screenshot name="docs-secure-dns" frame="rounded" maxWidth={600} alt="Settings › Privacy and protection › Secure DNS, set to Your network's DNS, with Encrypted, with fallback and Encrypted only below it" />

## Steps

1. Open Settings › Privacy and protection.
2. Under Secure DNS, choose **Encrypted, with fallback**.
3. In **Choose provider:**, choose a resolver.

To use encrypted DNS with no fallback, choose **Encrypted only** in step 2.

## Details

- **Your network's DNS.** This option works with internal names, VPNs, and
  router filters. Your network can see the sites you look up.
- **Encrypted, with fallback.** Sval looks sites up through the resolver.
  When that fails, Sval uses your network's DNS.
- **Encrypted only.** Sval looks sites up only through the resolver. If the
  resolver can't be reached, sites don't load.
- The resolvers are Quad9, which is the default, and Cloudflare. To use
  another resolver, choose **Custom** and enter its address.
- **Internal names.** Domains in this list always use your network's DNS,
  like names on a home or work network.
- Secure DNS applies to every site. The setting syncs to your other devices.
- Sval contacts only the resolver you choose.
