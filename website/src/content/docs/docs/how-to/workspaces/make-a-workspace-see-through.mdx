A see-through Workspace lets your desktop show through the whole window
around the page, tinted with the Workspace's color. The page itself stays
solid.

You keep a photo of the coast as your desktop picture, and you'd like to
see some of it around your Home Workspace's tabs.

<Screenshot name="docs-workspace-see-through" frame="rounded" alt="A see-through Home Workspace on a Mac. The purple desktop shows through the Workspace panel and the toolbar around a Wikipedia article." />

## Steps

1. Above your tabs, right-click the Workspace's name.
2. Choose **Edit…**.
3. Under **Color**, drag **Transparency** to the right.
4. Select **Save**.

## Details

- Transparency works on a Mac, on Windows 11, and on Linux on Wayland, in
  GNOME 51 and later or KDE Plasma 6.7 and later. On Windows 10, and on
  Linux desktops that can't blur what's behind a window, the window stays
  solid and the dialog has no Transparency.
- A Workspace with **No color** can be see-through too, in Sval's own
  window color.
- The window keeps at least a third of its color, so the Workspace's color
  always shows. Sval picks text that reads on that color alone, so a very
  light or very dark window behind Sval's window can make the text harder
  to read.
- On a Mac, turning on System Settings › Accessibility › Display › Reduce
  transparency makes the window solid and hides Transparency. On Windows,
  turning off Transparency effects in the Windows Colors settings does the
  same, and on Linux so does your desktop's high contrast
  setting.
- The New Workspace dialog has Transparency too, so you can set it when
  you make a Workspace.
