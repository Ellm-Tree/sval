A sleeping tab gives back the memory its page used. The tab stays in the
Workspace, dimmed. When you select the tab, its page loads again.

Your Work Workspace has a group of 20 Codeberg tabs you won't read again
until Monday.

<Screenshot name="docs-tab-menu" frame="rounded" maxWidth={216} alt="A tab's menu in the Workspace, from New tab to Close, with Put to Sleep just above Keep Awake and Close" />

## Steps

1. In the Workspace, right-click the tab or the group, then choose
   **Put to Sleep**.

When you put a group to sleep, every tab inside the group goes to sleep.
If you're viewing a tab that goes to sleep, Sval switches you to another
tab first.

## Details

- A sleeping tab's row is dimmed, with a gray icon.
- Some pages ask before you leave them, for example a page with text you
  haven't sent. Those pages also ask before they go to sleep.
- Put to Sleep skips tabs you've set to Keep Awake. Put to Sleep is dimmed
  when there's nothing to put to sleep.
- A sleeping tab keeps its place and its history. When you select a
  sleeping tab, its page reloads.
- Sval also puts tabs to sleep by itself in Workspaces you've left. See
  [When Sval puts tabs to sleep](/docs/explanation/sleeping-tabs/).
