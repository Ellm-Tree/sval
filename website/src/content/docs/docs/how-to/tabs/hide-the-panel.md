---
title: Hide the Workspace panel
description: Hide the Workspace panel to give the page the whole window, and show it again.
---

You can hide the Workspace panel and give the page the whole width of the window.

You're reading a long Codeberg diff and want every column of it on screen.

## Steps

1. Press <kbd>⌃Z</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl+Alt+Z</kbd> on Windows and Linux).

The Workspace panel slides away. To show it again, press the same keys. The
focus goes to the tab you're viewing.

You can also select **Workspaces** in the toolbar, or choose
**Hide Workspace Panel** in the menu bar's Workspaces menu.

## Reach your tabs while the Workspace panel is hidden

1. Move the pointer to the edge of the window on the Workspace panel's side,
   and hold it there.

The Workspace panel slides in over the page, and the page stays where it
is. When you select a tab, or move the pointer away from the Workspace
panel, the Workspace panel slides away again.

To keep the Workspace panel showing, press <kbd>⌃Z</kbd>
(<kbd>Ctrl+Alt+Z</kbd> on Windows and Linux) while it's in view.

## Details

- On a Mac, a web page can claim <kbd>⌃Z</kbd> for itself, the way a terminal in a
  page does. While that page has the focus, <kbd>⌃Z</kbd> doesn't hide the Workspace panel.
- You can change the keys in Settings › Keyboard shortcuts.
- Each window shows or hides its own Workspace panel.
- The Workspace panel stays in view while one of its menus is open, or
  while you rename something in the Workspace.
- In a full-screen window, move the pointer all the way to the edge of the
  screen.
- To stop the Workspace panel sliding in at the edge, turn off the switch in
  Settings › Appearance › Hidden Workspace panel.
