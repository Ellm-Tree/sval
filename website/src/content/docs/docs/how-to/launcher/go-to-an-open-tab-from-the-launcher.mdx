As you type, the Launcher lists your open tabs from every Workspace. Pick
a tab to go to it. If the tab is in another Workspace, Sval takes you to
that Workspace too.

You remember reading a Codeberg issue this morning, but not which Workspace
it's in, and you have 40 tabs open.

<Screenshot name="features-launcher" alt="The Launcher searching lisbon, with open tabs from other Workspaces, a Favorite, and history" />

## Steps

1. Press <kbd>⌘L</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl+L</kbd> on Windows and Linux).
2. Type a few letters of the tab's title or address.
3. Under Open tabs, select the tab.

The window shows the tab. If the tab is in another Workspace, the window
switches to that Workspace first.

## Details

- With nothing typed, the Launcher lists the tabs you used most recently,
  under Recent tabs.
- When a tab is in another Workspace, its row shows that Workspace's emoji
  and name, in the Workspace's color. The selected row reads Switch to and
  the Workspace's name.
<Until v="0.1.0-rc.10">

- A strong match for what you typed becomes the first row, so pressing
  Enter goes straight to it.

</Until>
<Since v="0.1.0-rc.10">

- The first row is always a search for what you typed, or the site when
  you type an address. When what you typed starts a word in a tab's title,
  or starts the tab's domain, Open tabs comes right after the first row.
  Press <kbd>↓</kbd>, then Enter, to go to the tab.

</Since>

- <kbd>Shift+Enter</kbd> and <kbd>⇧⌘Enter</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl+Shift+Enter</kbd>) also switch to the tab. They
  never open a second copy of the tab.
- <kbd>⌥Enter</kbd> (<kbd>Alt+Enter</kbd>) puts the tab next to the one you're on, in a Split.
  If the tab is in another Workspace, Sval moves it into this one first.
  Sval asks before it moves a tab into or out of an isolated Workspace.
- Typing % and a space lists every open tab.
- Open tabs lists up to 4 tabs while you type. To change the number, see
  [Change what the Launcher lists](/docs/how-to/launcher/change-what-the-launcher-lists/).
